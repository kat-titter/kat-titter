Professional Summary

Interdisciplinary scientist and engineer working at the interface of computational imaging, machine learning, and laboratory automation in biotechnology. Eight-plus years designing and scaling high-content / high-throughput biological assays and the image- and data-analysis systems that turn them into machine-learning training data. Founding member of the technical staff at an AI-driven drug-safety company; inventor of novel phenotypic bioactivity-detection methods; co-author of eight peer-reviewed papers in journals including Nature Communications, Cell Systems, iScience, the Journal of Biological Chemistry, and Molecular Neurodegeneration Advances, including studies establishing new benchmarks for small-molecule bioactivity prediction.

Selected Contributions of Major Significance

  • Invented two novel bioactivity (morphology-change) detection methods that sensitively detect small-molecule effects on cells at approximately 10× lower concentrations than conventional direct-toxicity assays (LDH, MT-Glo, cell count), expanding the sensitivity range of phenotypic safety screening.
  • Oversaw assay development and data generation for two of the world's largest Cell Painting datasets (100,000+ unique compounds), foundational training data for in silico toxicology models.
  • Co-developed an interpretable in silico clinical-risk model for drug-induced liver injury (DILI) reaching 0.92 AUC at 77% sensitivity and 90% specificity, matching or exceeding industry-standard in vitro assays (presented at the Society of Toxicology Annual Meeting, 2025).
  • Co-authored a field-influencing study showing that several widely used small-molecule bioactivity benchmarks are largely predicted by simply counting cells, reframing how the field validates phenotypic models (Nature Communications, 2026).
  • Optimized multiplexed expansion revealing (mExR) enabling simultaneous nanoscale imaging of 30+ proteins across 16× expanded brain tissue (published in Nature Communications, 2024).
